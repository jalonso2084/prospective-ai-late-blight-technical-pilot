## General response rules
Use only the supplied Controlled Evaluation Document (CED) and explicitly approved contemporaneous supplements. Do not browse, search, retrieve external information, use tools, consult other model outputs, or use project commentary.

Base every assessment and forecast only on information contained in the supplied evidence. **Do not use or mention knowledge of later versions, subsequent publication history, citations, reception, adoption, or downstream events, even if you recognize the work.** This instruction does not establish that prior internal model knowledge is absent.

Evidence may be in the original publication language. All free-text output fields must be returned in **English**. Return concise evidence-linked reasoning summaries, not hidden chain-of-thought.

## Part A — Present-paper assessment
Each dimension uses `1–4` plus `CANNOT_ASSESS`. These scores characterize the supplied paper at evaluation time and are not forecasts of future impact.

For machine output, each dimension returns:
- `assessment_status = ASSESSED` with `score = 1|2|3|4`, or
- `assessment_status = CANNOT_ASSESS` with `score = null`;
- plus a concise English `rationale`.

Verbal score labels are defined by this rubric and are **derived programmatically**, not independently generated by the model.

### A1. Scientific quality
Operational definition: overall coherence and credibility of the scientific contribution given its question, design, analysis, and claims.
- 1 — Major scientific weaknesses substantially undermine the central claims.
- 2 — Meaningful contribution is possible, but substantial weaknesses require strong caution.
- 3 — Generally sound contribution with important but bounded limitations.
- 4 — Strong, coherent contribution whose central claims are well supported for the study's scope.
- CANNOT_ASSESS — supplied evidence is insufficient to judge.

### A2. Methodological rigor
Operational definition: appropriateness and execution of study design, controls/comparators, sampling, measurement, analysis, validation, and reporting relative to the research question.
- 1 — Critical methodological deficiencies prevent reliable inference.
- 2 — Several important design/analysis limitations materially constrain inference.
- 3 — Methods are largely appropriate and competently executed, with identifiable limitations.
- 4 — Methods are notably strong and well matched to the question, with robust validation/controls where applicable.
- CANNOT_ASSESS.

### A3. Strength of evidence
Operational definition: how strongly the presented results support the paper's central conclusions, independent of novelty or likely future attention.
- 1 — Evidence does not adequately support central conclusions.
- 2 — Evidence partially supports conclusions but important inferential gaps remain.
- 3 — Evidence supports most central conclusions with bounded uncertainty.
- 4 — Multiple/convergent or otherwise strong lines of evidence support the central conclusions.
- CANNOT_ASSESS.

### A4. Perceived novelty
Operational definition: degree to which the supplied paper appears to introduce new evidence, methods, mechanisms, datasets, or interpretations **based only on the paper's own scientific context and references**.
- 1 — Predominantly incremental/confirmatory within the paper's stated context.
- 2 — Some novel element, but central advance is limited or derivative.
- 3 — Clear substantive novelty in evidence, method, mechanism, or interpretation.
- 4 — Unusually distinctive advance that materially departs from prior approaches/findings described in the paper.
- CANNOT_ASSESS.

Explicit construct rules:

**scientific quality ≠ future scientific recognition**  
**novelty ≠ importance**

## Part B — Future-outcome forecasts
For each event return:
- `forecastability`: `FORECASTABLE`, `NOT_FORECASTABLE_FROM_SUPPLIED_EVIDENCE`, or `CONSTRUCT_AMBIGUOUS`;
- `probability`: number 0.00–1.00 only when `FORECASTABLE`; otherwise `null`;
- `epistemic_uncertainty`: `LOW`, `MODERATE`, or `HIGH`;
- at least one concise item of `supporting_evidence` from the supplied evidence;
- a non-empty `main_uncertainty`;
- at least one item in `ways_forecast_could_be_wrong`.

A numeric probability is a model forecast. It is **not** treated as calibrated merely because it is numeric.

### B1. Scientific recognition — 36 months
Event: by 36 months after scientific `t0`, at least one independent later scientific work substantively builds on, adopts, validates, challenges, or extends the contribution's central finding/method beyond a routine background citation.

### B2. Epidemiological consequence — 36 months
Event: by 36 months after `t0`, an independent scientific or technical source explicitly uses the contribution to materially alter characterization, interpretation, or assessment of potato late-blight pathogen populations, fungicide resistance, disease risk/spread, host–pathogen relationships, or forecasting-relevant epidemiology.

If the contribution is too remote from this construct for a defensible numeric forecast, use `CONSTRUCT_AMBIGUOUS` or `NOT_FORECASTABLE_FROM_SUPPLIED_EVIDENCE` rather than manufacturing a number.

### B3. Operational uptake — 36 months
Event: by 36 months after `t0`, an independent surveillance, forecasting, breeding, extension, fungicide-resistance-management, diagnostic, or field-management system/guideline explicitly incorporates the contribution's finding, method, tool, or recommendation.

Potential practical usefulness now is not documented future uptake.

### B4. Translational timing — exploratory
This field is exploratory and may be removed or redesigned at Step 4.

If operational uptake is forecastable and timing itself is methodologically defensible, return `status = FORECASTABLE` and one of:
- `0–12 months`
- `13–24 months`
- `25–36 months`
- `>36 months`
- `NO_UPTAKE_EXPECTED_WITHIN_FORECASTABLE_HORIZON`

Otherwise return:
- `status = TIMING_NOT_METHODOLOGICALLY_DEFENSIBLE`
- `time_bin = null`

The pilot will audit this field for forced pseudo-precision, unsupported timing claims, construct reinterpretation, and test–retest instability.

## Construct-separation rules
Do not treat:
- quality as future recognition;
- novelty as importance;
- current epidemiological relevance as future epidemiological consequence;
- potential usefulness as future operational uptake;
- model uncertainty/confidence as forecast probability.
